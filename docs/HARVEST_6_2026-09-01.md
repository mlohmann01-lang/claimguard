# ClaimGuard / AutoClaim — Harvest 6

Date: 2026-09-01
Status: PROMOTED BUILD INPUT

## Harvest conclusion

ClaimGuard remains commercially interesting, but the market has moved. AI invoice extraction and validation for NDIS Plan Managers is now an active competitive category. Existing products already advertise OCR/AI extraction, dozens of checks, integration with plan-management platforms, direct claims workflows and approval-ready processing.

The strategic implication is not to abandon ClaimGuard. It is to move the differentiation up the stack.

## Competitive baseline

Observed market capabilities now include:

- invoice ingestion by email/upload;
- OCR/AI extraction;
- 50+ checks/rules;
- matching to plan-manager records;
- existing-platform integration;
- approval-ready invoice processing;
- direct claiming/auto-payment support in incumbent plan-management suites.

These capabilities are now table stakes rather than defensible product claims.

## Promoted differentiation

ClaimGuard should compete on:

- effective-date/versioned NDIS rules;
- decision provenance and reproducibility;
- evidence-aware PASS/WARN/BLOCK;
- authority/override governance;
- remediation orchestration;
- claim/outcome/payment lineage;
- learning from exception/remediation outcomes;
- audit/fiduciary proof;
- provider-side revenue assurance as a later extension.

## Plan Manager remains ICP #1

The canonical sequence remains Plan Manager first:

`Invoice → validation → claim decision → AutoClaim → claim outcome → remittance/payment → provider/reimbursement payment → reconciliation`.

Provider-side Get Me Paid is a future second product surface using the same transaction kernel, not a replacement ICP.

## Get Me Paid harvest

The provider-side opportunity is strongest when defined as revenue assurance rather than generic accounts receivable.

Canonical provider loop:

`DeliveredSupport → evidence complete? → NDIS rule/price/category valid? → invoice/claim ready? → correct counterparty/routing? → submitted/sent → expected payment → rejection/dispute/delay diagnosis → remediation/follow-up → collected → reconciled`.

The key advantage over generic AR is that the system understands NDIS-specific transaction validity and can intervene before an invalid invoice is sent as well as after payment fails.

Potential metrics:

- Revenue at Risk;
- Revenue Ready;
- Revenue Protected;
- Revenue Recovered;
- delivered-not-invoiced value;
- invoice-to-payment cycle;
- rejection/dispute rate;
- remediation success;
- manual-touch rate.

## Shared kernel doctrine

Plan Manager and Provider products may share:

- participant/plan/support semantics;
- provider identities;
- invoice/claim representations;
- rule versions/evaluations;
- evidence artifacts;
- exception/remediation patterns;
- payment/remittance concepts;
- outcome learning.

They must NOT share effective authority or tenant data implicitly. The same transaction can look different from the Plan Manager and Provider perspectives and must preserve role-specific duties and permissions.

## Product boundary

Do not expand into:

- participant goal/case management;
- clinical/therapy notes as a primary system;
- rostering/WFM;
- generic CRM;
- financial advice;
- generic NDIS practice management.

Integrate with these systems rather than replace them.

## Open-source / architecture harvest

Useful patterns to adopt from broader invoice/workflow ecosystems:

- deterministic rules separated from probabilistic extraction;
- typed canonical transaction objects;
- idempotent intake/submission;
- append-only audit/event ledgers;
- durable workflow/retry state machines;
- explicit human approval/override steps;
- evidence references rather than flattened explanations;
- versioned rules/configuration;
- remediation as a first-class case/object rather than a free-text note.

## Commercial thesis

The strongest Plan Manager conversation is not `we use AI to process invoices`.

It is:

> **How many invoices require human touch, how many exceptions are repeated, how many claims fail first pass, how long does remediation take, and can you prove why each claim was processed? ClaimGuard turns that into a governed claims operating system.**
